# v1.22.0 — Authority is lineage: mail in a hierarchy

> **Status:** implemented — v1.22.0. Design date: 2026-09-16. Base: v1.21.2. Prompted by the
> question "who does the controller mail in a nested hierarchy, and what is an instruction there?"

## The problem

Mail had one authority name, `controller`. In a hierarchical run a sub-orchestrator is a worker to
the controller and a controller to its own workers, but its mail arrived at them as `[peer]`
information, and their `--to controller` asks bypassed it and landed in the top controller's inbox
— exactly the micromanagement the strategy forbids ("wide and shallow", decisions through owners).

## Options weighed

1. **Convention only** — a lead's authority travels in the briefs it writes; its mail is
   information. Zero code, but the channel loses the meaning the journal already holds, and asks
   still go to the wrong desk.
2. **A second authority name** (`--as lead`) or per-subtree journals. More surface, a new record
   to keep consistent, and it breaks the one-flat-journal invariant the board is built on.
3. **Derive authority from lineage.** The dispatch event already says who dispatched whom once it
   carries `--by`. Everything else follows from that one field, and single-level runs (where
   `--by` is the controller) are byte-for-byte unchanged.

Option 3 has the smallest blast radius that actually fixes the problem: one new optional field, one
resolver, no new channel, no new record, two words in the WORKER block.

## Decisions

- `Stint.by` (default `controller`); `State.lead_of(agent)`; `State.resolve_to(sender, to)`.
- `controller` and `lead` are **relative** recipients: the sender's open dispatcher, else the
  controller. A returned lead is no lead.
- Tags: `[controller]` binds everywhere; `[lead x]` binds the agents x dispatched; `[peer]` is
  information. The inbox trailer says so; the WORKER block says so in two more words.
- Escalation by silence, not by copy: the controller sees a subtree's ask only when its lead has
  left it unread past `STALE_AFTER` — `!` in attention with the answer command ready, and a
  `board check` finding naming the lead.
- `board peers` groups by team (same dispatcher) and lists other subtrees under "elsewhere".
- Subtasks are dotted ids under their parent (`3.1`); `natural_key` sorts them there.
- Inside a `board launch` lane, `BOARD_AGENT` is exported and `--by` defaults to it, so an engine
  that dispatches its own workers is their lead without being told.
- The dispatch event's `actor` is the lead, so the journal attributes the transition honestly.

## Not done

No enforcement of who may run `dispatch` (the board has no actor identity; it never had). No
subtree-scoped broadcast (`--to all` stays all-hands; a lead mails its team by name). No nested
journals — one flat journal at any depth remains the invariant.
